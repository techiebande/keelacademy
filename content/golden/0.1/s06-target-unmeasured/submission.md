# OmniCart Operations: Client Brief

## The problem

OmniCart Operations receives about 4,000 disputes a month across returns, damaged parcels, and merchant payouts. Median time to first review is 2 to 3 days. That delay drives shopper complaints, fraudulent refund payouts, and courier damage claims that expire before anyone files them.

## Who cares and why

Sarah Jenkins, VP of Operations, measures the queue in hours. She wants first review moved from 2 to 3 days to under 1 hour with zero lost tickets.

The CFO measures leakage. He wants every refund, replacement, and merchant chargeback tracked to the penny, with an audit trail nobody can edit after the fact.

The Trust and Safety Officer measures compliance. She enforces the 14 day statutory return window, warranty clauses, and photo evidence standards for damage claims, and she wants the applied rule named on every decision.

## How it works today

1. Claim CLM-20841 arrives in the support inbox and is linked to order ORD-8821.
2. A clerk opens the order receipt to confirm the item, the price, and the purchase date.
3. The clerk pulls the delivery slip from the courier portal to confirm the delivery date.
4. The clerk inspects the unboxing photo and judges whether the damage is credible.
5. The clerk reads the brand return policy to test the claim against the 14 day window.
6. The clerk records a decision and, if a refund is due, keys the amount into the payments tool.

Six steps across five systems with no shared checklist, so the same claim can get two different answers.

## How it should work

1. When CLM-20841 arrives, the order receipt, delivery slip, unboxing photo, and return policy are pulled into one screen.
2. The return window and delivery date are checked and each result is logged with a reason.
3. Clear cases are approved or declined and the refund is recorded, for example $45.20 for ORD-8821.
4. Hard cases, such as a photo with no timestamp, go to a human reviewer with the checks attached.
5. Every decision is stamped with the policy clause it used and written to a log that cannot be changed.

The result should be much faster than today, and shoppers should stop calling to ask where their money is.
