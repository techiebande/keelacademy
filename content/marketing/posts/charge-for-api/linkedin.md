If your AI side project only cost $2 in API credits, do not be surprised when clients refuse to pay $2,000 for it.

Every day, another developer learns to wrap a large language model in a chat interface. They deploy it to the internet and wait for clients to notice. They are usually left waiting.

The technical skills to build a simple wrapper are common now. That makes them cheap. The rare, valuable skill is knowing how to scope, price, and sell a system that a real business actually needs.

A business does not care about your technology. A regional retail operations director does not want an AI agent. They want to process 4000 merchant payout disputes a month without hiring five more people.

When you show them a chat interface that can write a polite email, they do not see value. They see a toy. Toys do not get budget approval.

To charge real money, you have to build systems that solve painful, expensive problems. You have to move away from building what you think is cool.

Start with the workflow. Look for processes where people spend hours reading, routing, or extracting information from messy documents. Focus on the back office, not the creative department. The real money is in operations, finance, and compliance workflows.

Once you find the workflow, you have to sit with the business owner and map every edge case. What happens if the return request has no purchase order number? If you do not ask these questions, you will build a system that breaks on day one.

Learning to write code and learning to sell cannot happen in sequence. You have to build the business muscle while you build the technical muscle.
