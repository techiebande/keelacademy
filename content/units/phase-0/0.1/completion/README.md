# Your turn: write the OmniCart client brief

You just read a client brief for Apex Freight Logistics. Now you will write one for OmniCart. Use the facts below, follow the rules, and fill in the template. Plan for about 40 minutes of your hour. Do not use any tool but a plain text editor and your own head. To count words, paste your text into any word counter, or count about ten words per line.

## What OmniCart told us

- OmniCart Operations is a 120-person online store and shipping hub. It sells goods from hundreds of brands.
- Each month, OmniCart gets about 4,000 return requests, damaged parcel claims, courier delivery slips, and payout disputes.
- Today, each case waits 2 to 3 days before a clerk looks at it.
- The target is a decision in under 1 hour, with zero lost tickets.
- Sarah Jenkins is the VP of Operations. She wants the wait cut from 2 to 3 days to under 1 hour, and no ticket lost.
- A ticket is one case in the queue. A claim, a return request, and a dispute are all tickets.
- The CFO wants every refund tracked in whole cents, in a record no one can change.
- The Trust and Safety Officer wants every return to follow the 14 day return rule. Every damage claim must have photo proof.
- To decide a case, a clerk reads four papers. They are the order receipt, the delivery slip, the unboxing photo, and the return policy.
- The delivery slip is the courier's note saying when the parcel was delivered.
- The unboxing photo is the picture the shopper takes when opening the parcel.
- Money is always written in whole cents. So 45.20 dollars is written as 4520 cents.
- Every claim has a claim number like CLM-20841. Every order has an order number like ORD-8821.

## The rules

1. Your brief must be 250 to 500 words.
2. Use these five headings, spelled exactly like this and in this order:
   `# OmniCart Operations: Client Brief`, `## The problem`, `## Who cares and why`, `## How it works today`, `## How it should work`.
   The # marks are part of the heading. Type them exactly as shown, then one space, then the words.
3. Under The problem, write three sentences or fewer. Name two numbers: about 4,000 a month, and the 2 to 3 day wait.
4. Under Who cares and why, write one sentence per leader, with or without a bullet mark in front. The three sentences must each want a different thing.
5. Under How it works today, write 4 to 7 numbered steps. Start when the box with the shopper's note arrives at the dock. End at the refund paid or denied. Name all four papers: the order receipt, the delivery slip, the unboxing photo, and the return policy.
6. Under How it should work, write 4 to 7 numbered steps. Start when the box with the shopper's note arrives at the dock. End at the refund paid or denied. State the target wait of under 1 hour inside this section.
7. Write at least one amount in whole cents, such as 4520 cents.
8. In How it should work, a person must still review the hard cases.
9. Do not use these words: AI, agent, LLM, model, prompt, automation. One hit and the brief fails. Say what should happen, not what tool does it.

A step may be one or two sentences, so you do not need to pad.

## The five checks

Your brief is graded on five checks, and all five must pass.

1. The brief uses none of the technology words listed in rule 9.
2. The problem gives the volume and the wait in three sentences or fewer.
3. Three leaders each want a different thing.
4. Today's steps are 4 to 7, in order, and name the four papers.
5. The target steps are 4 to 7, with under 1 hour, a person on hard cases, and whole cents.

The other rules help you pass these five.
