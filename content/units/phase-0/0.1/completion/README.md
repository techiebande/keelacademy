# Your turn: write the OmniCart client brief

Now you will write the client brief for OmniCart Operations.
Use the facts below, follow the rules, and fill in the template.
Plan for about 40 minutes of your hour.
Use only a plain text editor and your computer file manager.
To count words, paste your text into a word counter or count words per line.

## What OmniCart told us

- OmniCart Operations is an online store and shipping hub with 120 people.
- Each month, OmniCart receives about 4,000 requests, claims, slips, and disputes.
- Today, each case waits 2 to 3 days before a clerk reviews it.
- The target is a decision in under 1 hour, with zero lost tickets.
- A ticket is one customer case in the work queue.
- Sarah Jenkins is the VP of Operations.
- She wants the wait cut from 2 to 3 days to under 1 hour, and no ticket lost.
- The CFO wants every refund tracked in whole integer cents, like 4520 cents.
- The CFO also wants records that nobody can change after review.
- The Trust and Safety Officer enforces the 14 day return rule.
- The Trust and Safety Officer also requires photo proof for damage claims.
- A clerk reviews four documents to settle each claim.
- The four documents are the order receipt, the delivery slip, the unboxing photo, and the return policy.
- The order receipt lists what the customer bought and paid.
- The delivery slip is the courier note confirming delivery date and time.
- The unboxing photo is the picture taken by the customer when opening the parcel.
- The return policy sets the return rules for each product category.
- Every claim has a claim id like CLM-20841.
- Every order has an order id like ORD-8821.

## The rules

1. Your brief must be 250 to 500 words.
   To reach this range comfortably, write 1 to 2 clear sentences per step.
   Explain what is checked or done in each step.
2. Use five required headings in exact order.
   The title is `# OmniCart Operations: Client Brief`.
   Section one is `## The problem`.
   Section two is `## Who cares and why`.
   Section three is `## How it works today`.
   Section four is `## How it should work`.
   The # marks are part of each heading.
   Your deliverable title is OmniCart Operations: Client Brief.
3. Under The problem, write three sentences or fewer.
   State the volume of about 4,000 requests each month and the 2 to 3 days wait.
4. Under Who cares and why, write three lines for the three leaders.
   Name the VP of Operations, the CFO, and the Trust and Safety Officer.
   Give each leader a different goal.
5. Under How it works today, write 4 to 7 numbered steps.
   Trace from dock arrival to the final payout decision.
   Name the order receipt, the delivery slip, the unboxing photo, and the return policy.
6. Under How it should work, write 4 to 7 numbered steps.
   Use the same start and finish as the current process.
   State the target review time of under 1 hour.
7. In How it should work, send hard cases to a person for review.
8. Under How it should work, write at least one money amount in whole integer cents, like 4520 cents.
9. Do not use these words: AI, agent, LLM, model, prompt, automation, software, algorithm, python, docker, api, database, json, schema, pipeline, embedding, token, chatbot, machine learning.
   One hit and the brief fails.
   Say what happens, not what technology does it.
10. Use clean plain language.
    Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or exclamation marks.
    Do not use corporate buzzwords like leverage, streamline, or seamless.

## The five checks

Your work is graded on five checks, and all five must pass.

1. The brief contains none of the banned technology words.
2. The problem states the volume and the wait in three sentences or fewer.
3. Three leaders each want a different outcome.
4. Today's steps are 4 to 7, in order, and name all four documents.
5. Target steps are 4 to 7, with under 1 hour, a person on hard cases, and whole cents.

The other rules help you pass these five checks.
