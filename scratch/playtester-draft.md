# OmniCart Operations: Client Brief

## The problem

OmniCart Operations handles about 4,000 return requests, damage claims, and disputes each month. Customer cases wait 2 to 3 days on the intake dock before a clerk begins review. Frustrated shoppers send complaints while damaged goods pile up across the warehouse floor.

## Who cares and why

1. Sarah Jenkins, VP of Operations: wants triage cycle time cut from 2 to 3 days to under 1 hour with zero lost tickets in the queue.
2. The CFO: wants every refund tracked in whole integer cents with an audit trail that nobody can alter after approval.
3. The Trust and Safety Officer: enforces the 14 day return policy and requires unboxing photo evidence for all damage claims.

## How it works today

1. A courier drops returned parcels at the warehouse intake dock, where workers scan package labels into the receiving queue.
2. Each parcel sits untouched in the intake queue for 2 to 3 days before a clerk opens the ticket.
3. The clerk pulls the order receipt from the sales ledger to check purchase dates and the total amount paid.
4. The clerk verifies shipment tracking details and arrival dates against the courier delivery slip.
5. The clerk opens the customer claim file to inspect the unboxing photo for visible damage.
6. The clerk checks product warranty rules and the 14 day return policy to confirm return eligibility.
7. The clerk calculates the payout on paper, rounds the amount to whole dollars, and submits the refund for manual payment.

## How it should work

1. A courier drops returned parcels at the intake dock, where intake clerks log claim numbers into the queue.
2. A reviewer cross-references the order receipt and the courier delivery slip to confirm delivery dates.
3. The reviewer checks the unboxing photo against the 14 day return policy to verify return eligibility.
4. Clean intake cases finish review in under 1 hour from dock arrival to payout resolution.
5. If papers do not match or evidence is unclear, a human specialist inspects the claim file.
6. The reviewer approves the exact refund amount in integer cents, recording 4520 cents on the ledger without rounding.
7. The finance desk issues the customer refund and stores a permanent record that nobody can change.
